# Costing Framework

## Purpose

This document defines how EasePal Care costs will be identified, classified, and modelled.

## Principle

We will not begin with arbitrary financial figures.

We will begin with the activities required to operate the model and identify the costs generated by those activities.

## Cost Driver Categories

Every cost should be classified into one of the following:

### 1. Per CHEW

Costs that increase directly with the number of CHEWs.

Examples:

- Recruitment
- Training
- Equipment
- Uniform
- Device
- CHEW remuneration
- CHEW logistics

### 2. Per Patient

Costs that increase directly with the number of patients.

Examples:

- Patient consumables
- Patient-specific services
- Certain care-delivery costs

### 3. Per LGA / Location

Costs associated with establishing or operating in a geographic area.

Examples:

- Community entry
- Stakeholder engagement
- Local deployment
- Location-specific supervision

### 4. Fixed / Shared

Costs that support the overall programme and do not increase directly with every CHEW.

Examples:

- Management
- Core technology
- Office
- Finance
- Legal
- Administration

### 5. Percentage-Based

Costs calculated as a percentage of another cost or revenue base.

Examples:

- Contingency
- Payment processing
- Certain administrative provisions

## Cost Lifecycle

Costs will also be considered across the lifecycle:

1. Planning
2. Recruitment
3. Training
4. Onboarding
5. Equipment
6. Deployment
7. Service delivery
8. Supervision
9. Monitoring and evaluation
10. Administration
11. Business development
12. Replacement and contingency

## Rule

No item should be placed in "Miscellaneous" merely because its cost category is unclear.

Unclassified costs should first be investigated and assigned to the appropriate cost driver.
